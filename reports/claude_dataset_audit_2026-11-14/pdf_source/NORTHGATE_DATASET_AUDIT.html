1. Executive summary

What I did. I read the project documents and the original 30-page brief (found in your Downloads folder), read every script and the Kaggle notebook, recomputed your features, labels and splits from the raw files, checked all eleven NSE files you downloaded against Yahoo, rebuilt the CPI tables from the eighteen government workbooks, ran the tests, and listed every file with its fingerprint (hash). I changed nothing in your project.

The five things to know.

  1. Your numbers check out. 29,484 price rows, 29,473 labelled rows and the 21,708 / 6,379 / 1,386 split all reproduce exactly. The saved files are bit-for-bit what the scripts produce.
  2. The Yahoo-versus-NSE gap is explained. Yahoo rewrites old prices every time a company later has a split, bonus, rights issue or (usually) a demerger. On all 110 stock-days I could compare, Yahoo equals the official NSE price multiplied by the factors of later events, with no remainder. For days with no later event, Yahoo equals NSE exactly.
  3. The data contains real errors. Nineteen rows are copies of the previous day's price with zero volume: ten on a day the exchange was closed (15 Jan 2026) and nine on a normal trading day (18 Mar 2025). At least seven real trading sessions are missing. Two labels are distorted by demergers (ITC, Hindustan Unilever).
  4. The first model experiment is not reproducible from the repository. It ran on a different dataset from the one on your Mac, with code edited inside the Kaggle notebook and never committed. Its results exist only as text printed in that notebook unless the Kaggle files are downloaded.
  5. Most of the brief is still ahead. Macro data beyond a partial inflation table, all news and sentiment, portfolio optimisation, backtesting, recommendations, the dashboard and the report are not started. My estimate of the remaining work is seven to eight weeks for one person. The recorded deadline is 15 November 2026.

5.2 How Yahoo's prices relate to official NSE prices

Think of five different "prices" for the same day: (1) the price actually traded, (2) that price rescaled for later splits, bonuses and rights, (3) rescaled again for later dividends, (4) a full "what did a shareholder earn" series, and (5) a series using only what was known on that day. Yahoo's Close is type 2, Adj Close is type 3. You hold type 1 for only 11 days. Types 4 and 5 do not exist in the project.

ExampleNSE closeYahoo closeRatioExplanation

RELIANCE 13 May 20201,496.45690.610.461500.923 (2023 demerger) × 0.5 (2024 bonus)

What this means for you: daily returns from Yahoo are sound for model training except around the two demergers; price levels are not what was seen at the time and should not be model inputs. Do not "undo" Yahoo by doubling or by a single multiplier; the factor differs by date and by stock.

5.3 Inflation (CPI) data

The work is careful and correct: values, provisional/final markers and actual publication times are preserved, and each release is used only from the next trading day. Two limits stop it being useful yet. It begins in August 2024, after the training period ends, so no model can learn from it. And it ends with December 2025, after which the same value is repeated for 300 days. One of the saved files is also damaged (three records run together) and cannot be opened by standard tools; the content is recoverable.

Academic papersTwo reviewed papers study monthly returns across thousands of stocks. Useful for method discipline; their results do not transfer to a ten-stock daily study.
First experimentStructure was sound (five time-ordered folds, no test data). It is best labelled "exploratory": untuned models, one random seed, flawed data rows, and a headline average dominated by the two highest-priced stocks (41% of the error comes from LT and TCS).
About "no model beat the simple baseline". Predicting that tomorrow's price equals today's is very hard to beat for next-day prices, and the brief treats reporting this honestly as a valid outcome. The first experiment neither proves the models useless nor shows any edge. The differences seen (0.7% to 1.7% of average error) are too small to interpret without uncertainty ranges. One correction to the notes: the baseline's "0.5% directional accuracy" is simply the share of days with exactly zero change, because a forecast of "no change" was scored as neither up nor down.
